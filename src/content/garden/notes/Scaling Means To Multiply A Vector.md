To "Scale" a vector means to multiply it by a variable. Effectively, what you are doing when scaling a vector is to stretch it out over a graph by the variable.

The variables in linear algebra that you are multiplying by are called "Scalars". 

![[scaling_a_vector.webp]]

**Some Examples:**

$\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} \times 2 = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 4 \end{bmatrix}$

$\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} \times 1/3 = \begin{bmatrix} 0.33 \\ 1.33 \end{bmatrix}$

$\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} \times -1 = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ -2 \end{bmatrix}$

Notice in the last example that the negative is applied to both coordinates in the vector. That is because scaling by a negative effectively reverses the course of the directions.
