# Trusting AI Is Opposite To Control

Many of the greatest people that have ever existed became great and achieved the feats that they are famous for because they refused to give up. They had a belief or desire, and they had the conviction to continually and obsessively pursue the manifestation of that desire into existence. In essence, despite every challenge faced along the way, they refused to be told “no”.
Yet, with AI, as we spend trillions of CapEx on the creation of unnatural, super intelligence, we have ongoing safety debates about how we can understand and control this AI.
Of course we want alignment. We don’t want to create something more intelligent than us that also hates us. We would prefer the AI to act as a benevolent, wise parent or council, than a moody and spiteful teenager. 
But this is not the same as needing to control it. And if we were to achieve a super intelligent machine that we could control, how useful would it really be?
The brilliance of those who refused to be told “no” was that they saw a way to make the world in a different way and more importantly than that, did not let the judgement of the crowd infiltrate their passion to succeed.
If another had control over these people, it is almost guaranteed that the benefits that came with their fury of work would not be felt by us today.
If we do succeed in super intelligence, the goal should be that we can trust it,  not control it.
