## Takeaways

Most people buy businesses like they are lottery tickets. But they are businesses.

> [[Investing In A Stock Is Investing In A Business]]

This costs them more than just the money they lose. 

> [[The Cost Of Underperformance Is Worse Than Just Losing Money]]

So what should we do about that?

First, be humble. Recognize your inability to predict the future and let that dictate your strategy.

> [[Accept You Don't Know The Future And Let That Guide You]]

Okay, I am humble. Now what?

If you recognize the fact that you are becoming an owner in a business, it follows that success in markets comes from thinking like business owner.

#### Principal 1: Know your business

> [[Investing In A Stock Is Investing In A Business]]

#### Principal 2: Only you know what is best for your money

> [[Investing In A Stock Is Investing In A Business]]

Note that this means being cautious and judgemental of the management of a business. Just as you wouldn't hand over your life savings to a shady wealth manager, don't invest in businesses with management that have bad reputations, or that you just don't like.

On that note, if performing a valuation, your judgement of management should not be represented in the valuation, or the discount rate. It is already baked into the historical performance of the business, which is your valuation baseline. 

> [[Including Management's Abilities In Your Valuation Is Double Counting]]

#### Principal 3: Make sure the price of the asset is reasonable from an owner perspective

> [[Investing In A Stock Is Investing In A Business]]

Stock prices explained through Mr Market analogy

> [[Mr Market Is An Irrational Partner]]

So how do we know if a stock is over priced and thus dangerous? Some quick rules:

1. Avoid IPOs

> [[Avoid IPOs, Always]]

2. Set a maximum limit on the PE ratio you will accept

> [[Set A Maximum Limit On The PE Ratio You Will Accept]]

3. Sell offs do not equal value (value traps)

> [[Sell-offs Do Not Equal Value]]

4. Margin of safety

> [[A Margin Of Safety Is The Most Important Tool For The Protective Investor]]

Alright, this seems hard. But I understand how to think and what not to do. What _should_ I do?

#### Principal 4: Trust your judgement

If an investment is just becoming the owner of a business. Its pretty intuitive to think that you probably just want to own the best businesses. Why own a local sushi restaurant when you can own Apple? Some businesses just have characteristics that make them *durable*.

> [[Companies Resemble Natural Living Organisms]]

This also goes against the traditional advice of being diversified.

[[Diversification Is The Opposite Of Staying In Your Circle Of Competence]]

To find them we just have to do what we've already discussed. Do the work to find and understand them. Then, just wait for the market to offer you a great price.

> [[Sit On Your Ass Investing]]

And in the end, you can only trust yourself. The musings of the market don't matter in the slightest.

>[[Investing In A Stock Is Investing In A Business]]
