## Takeaways

The P/E ratio of Amazon has been above thirty since 2006. 

![[amzn_pe_ratio.webp]]

When you include years with near-zero or negative earnings, the ratio becomes even more extreme. In fact, versus the S&P500, Amazon's average valuation has been about ten times more expensive than the index for thirty years.

![[WhereTheMoneyIs_AmazonValuationVsSnP500.webp]]

For most value investors, ever looking for a bargain, or at least a fair price, the stock has always looked like one to avoid. Other metrics favoured by so called "intelligent investors" have told a similar story. GAAP profits and returns on invested capital have looked negative many times in Amazon's history. Yet, since 2006, Amazon is up ~12,600%, while the S&P500 is up only ~440%. 

![[amzn_vs_sp500_returns.webp]]

With two decades of underperformance behind them, any honest Warren Buffett disciple  has to ask themselves where they went wrong. After all, Jeff Bezos himself is a massive fan of Buffett and frequently discussed concepts familiar to value investors in his annual letter to shareholders. With hindsight it is perfectly clear that Amazon was a value play all along, and Bezos, along with the investors who called right, reaped massive reward. But how did they know? Sophisticated investors don't gamble. They don't just throw money to the next new technology, or hot stock. They are thoughtful and systematic. The problem is that the traditional system taught to investors by Benjamin Graham, Buffet and Munger, doesn't seem to work anymore. Is this the end of value investing, or, is it something else? Is it the end of the discipline that has brought so many successful investors enormous wealth, or is it that our toolbox just needs an overhaul?  

This is the question Adam Seessel raises in his book *Where The Money Is*, and he suspects the answer is the later. After all, Bezos understands value and how to measure a businesses performance. So how did he so successfully steer the ship of Amazon for thirty years despite many of the common indicators flashing red? What can investors do, seeking to keep the core tenants of value investing alive, in order to thrive in what has come to be known of value 3.0, value investing in the digital age?
