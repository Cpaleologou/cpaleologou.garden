The first intuition of investors is that massive capital expenditure is to be followed by massive earnings growth as that capital is "turned on" and used for business operations.
A paper in the Journal of Finance, however, found the opposite to be true, and dubbed it the "asset-growth anomaly". Asset expansion activities, such as M&A, equity issuance and new loans, tend to be followed by low returns. 
The anomaly is that the opposite is also true. Asset contracting activities, such as buy backs, debt prepayments, spin-offs, and dividends initiation, are often followed by positive returns.
